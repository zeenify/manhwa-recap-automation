# NARRATION STYLE NOTES — from a real 636K-sub manhwa recap channel

Source: full transcript of a "Manhwa Fresh" recap video (saved in this session's
research; excerpts below are verbatim). This is the style bible for writer agents.

## The rules, learned from the transcript

1. **Fully diegetic narration.** The story is told as if it is happening. Words that
   NEVER appear: panel, sound effect, narrator, beat, caption, montage, chapter
   counting. Scene transitions use only: "the scene cuts to…", "the scene takes us
   to…", "Let's zoom out and explore…" (all appear in the real transcript).
2. **Jokes live inside the story voice.** They roast characters and situations, never
   the artwork or production. Verbatim examples:
   - "Truck Kun trolled his ass and sent him inside the game world right after he quit."
   - "It's basically the Harvard of this world, except talent is trained to be the best at killing monsters."
   - "This man's IQ must be room temperature because godamn what happens if it's not the real thing."
   - "his stamina is what you'd expect from a gamer virgin"
   - "the shittest skill of all time… It's an insult to call this ability a skill. It's more like a disability or a delusion at this point."
3. **Source-reader confidence.** The narrator knows every name, role, and
   relationship from the first line, and drops future knowledge as flavor:
   - "Elod Dez Reich, an overpowered presence in the game with all the talent in the world."
   - "this branch will one day be wielded by the hero of the story."
   - "Elodie is a helpful ally who plays a critical role in the game supporting the protagonist in the future."
4. **Recurring address vocabulary, used heavily:** "our boy," "our guy," "my guy,"
   "bro," "Bro's luck is in the gutter." ("Bro" even starts sentences.)
5. **Personal asides are rare and one line max:**
   - "And my god, I would have appreciated this skill during my exams."
   - "It kind of reminds me of the liquid gallium from the Terminator movies, except this time it's Pepsi colored."
6. **Dialogue is folded into narration** with quick tags:
   - "he asks — politely! — 'Oh! Ramen? May I have just one bite?'"
   - "our boy shuts all of them up by hammering the correct answer…"
7. **Cold-open structure confirmed:** the video opens on a strong mid-story scene
   ("Frondier is just walking through the academy hall when he sees the hottest and
   most talented student…"), then pivots to setup with a series-voice line:
   "Welcome to the most difficult game in the whole world."
8. **Casual profanity is part of the register** ("get his ass kicked," "shittest
   skill") — our voice can match this level, not exceed it.

## Pipeline consequence (the "context" fix)

The sample narrator narrates with *source-reader omniscience*. Our writer agent
cannot know who the MC is from the opening panels — so:
- The READER records only what is visually certain and flags ambiguity
  ("unclear: who the bubble tail points to").
- The WRITER does a **harmonization pass** after drafting: using the whole
  chapter's events, it retro-fixes early lines with learned names/roles, corrects
  action attributions, and neutralizes anything still ambiguous.
- Attribution rule for narration: when the panels don't make the actor certain, the
  narration stays neutral ("one of them hawks up…") — never assert what we can't see.
