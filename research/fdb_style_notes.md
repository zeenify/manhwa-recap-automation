## Per-video notes with evidence

### 1. Horror Movies I Could EASILY Survive (the flagship bit)

Opens with a personal confession that flips halfway:

> you know horror movies were a big part of my childhood and not cuz I used to
> watch them — on the contrary I used to avoid them at all cost... I was a
> scaredy-cat I'm not going to lie. But I think what scared me the most back then
> was the fact that if I was in any of those movies I would not survive... but
> now as a full-fledged grown 21-year-old I actually believe I have a pretty good
> shot against most of these horror movies. And yeah, I said MOST of them.

Premise = he is unbeatable and will prove it with "strategies." Per-villain
skeleton: introduce threat sincerely → absurd plan → pre-argue with the audience →
contingency → escalation to ridiculous.

> that's the dumbest thing they could do cuz what if the other one falls asleep,
> you're done. Draw two for your mistake, buddy. They had it all wrong. I have
> two solutions for Freddy: first... move out of Ohio, which when I say it out
> loud it's not that hard cuz who wants to live in Ohio. And two, the easier one:
> I just simply won't sleep... it's simple. I don't sleep REGARDLESS.

> I'll just pull him out of my dream and have someone with a shotgun there
> waiting for him. And if a shotgun don't work, then a shotgun with holy water.
> He not beating that.

Signature self-interruption — his own plan collapses mid-sentence and he pivots
without shame:

> the easiest method: just drive out. Just hop in, start the car... uh, I can't
> drive. Yeah, I'll just shoot his mom.

Demanding credit from the audience as a bit:

> y'all cannot tell me that's not a W strategy. Go ahead. [holds ear to mic]
> Say it. Say it.

And one honest surrender played as a betrayal apology:

> I am not surviving a single Saw movie. I'm sorry. I'm sorry.

Threat intro is a genuine power rundown, then the punch undercuts it:

Fake logic chains carried to a real conclusion:

> if there's an Armory, then there's guns, and where there's guns there's room
> for an American.

> I sleep with a shotgun under my pillow, a Glock in my sock, and an AK in my
> PJs.

His Hunger Games plan is the masterclass in bit-commitment: fake credentials
("I'm a hide-and-seek champion... people would give up on finding me and just go
home and NOT TELL ME for hours"), fake precision ("I've played thousands of
matches... and won a good portion of them — 17 to be specific"), and acting out
the movie's scene with a mundane capper ("3, 2, 1 — wait, are we eating them on
'go' or on 'one'? — I did it. I won. It's that easy, my people.").

### 3. Horror Movies That ACTUALLY Scared Me 2 (narrative recap mode)

This is the video closest to recap narration: he retells movie plots at length.
Relatable-observation opener, hyperbolic self-deprecation, and the pre-empted
objection:

> You know, I think we can all agree a lot of horror movies just aren't that
> scary. But lying beneath all this mediocre, not scary stuff are gems...
> It's been about 42 years since I did the last one, but today I'll be going
> over some more horror movies that I actually find scary. Yes. Me. I, Derek.
> Horror movies that I find scary. Had to make that clear.
> And in the end, I'll go over one of the scariest movies I've ever seen.
> All right, let's run it.

His recap rhythm: fast casual summary, then SLOW DOWN and zoom on the one detail
that matters, then interrogate it:

> Like Finny will be munching on his cereal, minding his business. "You think
> you could slurp that any louder?" Like damn, bro. It's the '70s. Let him enjoy
> his crunch logs in peace. Mind you, this is all only like 12 minutes into the
> movie.

The fake-out (setup promises one thing, short sentence detonates it):

> So when she gets wind of Kate's plan... she decides to handle it in a very
> calm and tactful way. Nah, I'm just kidding. She kills her.

The running refrain that structures the whole video ("There is something wrong
with this little girl" appears 3+ times), and escalation connectors between
scenes: "But to top it all off...", "And it doesn't even end there, cuz...",
"But that's not even the full explanation cuz...".

He voices characters constantly, quoting lines and replying to them:

> So she pulls up on him and tells him he needs to take some accountability —
> which is facts. That's facts. — by putting the bird out its misery. Oh my
> goodness. That's cold. But you are NINE years old. What are you talking about?

And where something genuinely scares him, the jokes stop:

> And when I tell y'all I've never been so scared to see a door in my life, I'm
> not trolling. Like that junk had my heart racing... I was so scared my friend
> had to let me know to uncover my ears cuz I was yelling.

Fake-philosophical opener and the numeric-escalation structure:

> You know, in this life, for everything good, there's something bad. Including
> movies. ... But are these reviews legit? ... Well, that's what we find out

Ratings become the segue engine (countdown structure):

> I'm giving it a 3.2 out of 10. I know that was low... but that's the highest
> one of the video. Next one is much worse. Coming in at a staggering 1.4 stars.
> Not 1.5. 1.4.

His best sustained joke grammar — the rhetorical interrogation of plot logic,
escalating until the bit becomes a character:

> Why does the ghost do this? Why does it need a missed phone call to kill
> people? ... But then I remembered it freaking killed the cat earlier. Am I
> supposed to believe the cat got a phone call too? ... The cat ain't have a
> phone, bro. But even if it did, what would a cat be doing that it can't pick
> up the phone? It's a cat. All they do is sleep, clean themselves, eat, and
> sleep. It's not exactly a tight schedule. So either this is the busiest cat in
> the world, or that ghost don't follow its own rules. This busy-ass cat. Who IS
> this cat?

He also invents dialogue for real people as a roast ("someone on set really
said, 'Oh, put her in a black hoodie so they can know she's evil. You're a
genius, bro.'") and closes with the playful sub/community ask:

> I'm gonna start streaming on Twitch soon... so if you want to hear me piss my
> pants live, now's your chance. Let's see if we can get to maybe a million
> followers by tomorrow. I know y'all can do it.

## Structural analysis

### Opening hook
Always personal, never "welcome back to the channel." Five of five videos open
with "You know, ..." followed by a claim about HIMSELF or a fake-universal truth,
then a twist ("on the contrary...", "low key though I could do that", "Including
movies."). The premise statement names the game ("today, ladies and gentlemen, we
will be going over..."), sets the bit's rules ("I'll give them my own rating",
"y'all know how we do it"), plants a saved-for-last escalation ("And in the end,
I'll go over the scariest/worst..."), and launches with "All right, let's run it."

### Premise framing
He commits to a ridiculous premise with total confidence and never breaks
character inside a segment: he is an immortal survival expert, a fair judge, a
brave horror veteran. The comedy comes from treating the absurd premise with
analyst seriousness — numbered solutions, contingencies, stat breakdowns. When
reality defeats him, he apologizes to the audience like an athlete who lost
("I'm sorry guys, I'm sorry, but I'll make it up to you. I'll definitely beat the
next one.").

### Joke grammar (the core patterns)
1. **Analyst setup → deadpan absurd verdict.** Sincere threat assessment, then a
   conclusion no analyst would reach ("I just simply won't sleep... it's simple").
2. **Pre-arguing with the audience.** "You're probably thinking X. Don't be
   silly." / "Let me get to that, okay." / "I bet y'all forgot about that." He
   voices the objection, dismisses it, then answers it.
3. **Rhetorical question chains.** Interrogate plot logic 4–6 questions deep,
   escalating until the questions become the joke (the busy cat).
4. **Fake-out detonation.** Long sincere setup, then a short reversal sentence:
   "Nah, I'm just kidding. She kills her."
5. **Self-interruption collapse.** His plan or sentence breaks mid-air and he
   shrugs into a worse plan ("uh, I can't drive. Yeah, I'll just shoot his mom.").
6. **Escalation triples.** "a shotgun under my pillow, a Glock in my sock, and an
   AK in my PJs." Third item is always the biggest.
7. **Fake precision.** Absurd exactness sells the bit: "17 to be specific,"
   "1.4 stars, not 1.5, 1.4," "I'll say five."
8. **Fake credentials.** Embarrassing personal "evidence": track team (kicked off
   for being too fast), three divorces on Sims, hide-and-seek champion, "I played
   Fortnite so I'm pretty sure I can figure it out."
9. **Acting out scenes.** He voices both sides of movie dialogue, then punctures
   the drama with a mundane question or reaction.
10. **Repeat-for-rhythm.** Doubled short sentences as emphasis: "I'm sorry. I'm
    sorry." / "Y'all going to see. Y'all going to see." / "Take it all in."
11. **The "pause" beat.** He flags his own dirty joke and refuses to elaborate:
    "I'm not pausing that... okay, I'll pause that one."
12. **Demanding his credit.** After landing a plan: "y'all cannot tell me that's
    not a W strategy. Say it. Say it."

### How he voices characters and dialogue
Dialogue is quoted in a mocking-but-affectionate impression, almost always with a
reaction tag after it ("which is facts. That's facts." / "I'm not even joking,
that's actually how he sounds to me."). He also writes NEW dialogue for
characters — imagining the killer planning his victim list, the voice actor
getting directing notes — and performs it. Trailing off mid-quote is itself a
punchline: "So she pulls up on her: 'Esther, when two people love each other
very much. You know, they they want to show they...' Yeah, I know. I know."

### Sentence rhythm
Breathless medium-length runs connected by "cuz" and "like," punctuated by very
short landing sentences AFTER the setup: "It's that easy." / "Surely." / "That's
right." / "Boom." / "Showtime." / "Disgusting." Punchlines land on short
sentences or stressed single words; setups ramble. He also front-loads a video
with energy and lets the last third breathe — the sincere beat usually arrives
right before the outro.

### Segues (exact phrases observed)
- Between list items: "first up I got..." / "all right, next one, for sure" /
  "now this one — this one is the one that you guys are going to underestimate" /
  "now I'm going to be very honest..." / "And now for the scariest movie of the
  video. It's no other than..."
- Rank-ladder (scared series): "It's not even close to the scariest horror movie
  I've watched. A movie that's a bit closer, though, is Orphan."
- Countdown (ratings series): "that's the highest one of the video. Next one is
  much worse. Coming in at a staggering 1.4 stars..."
- Community (submissions): "This next one comes in from [name] on YouTube/Discord/IG..."
- Between scenes within one story: "But to top it all off..." / "And it doesn't
  even end there, cuz..." / "But that's not even the full explanation cuz..." /
  "And this last thing she did had me genuinely scared." / "Now fast forward
  to..." / "This is where the horror really starts, cuz..."

### List vs narrative handling
List videos run an unspoken identical skeleton per item (intro → evidence →
roast → verdict) that he stretches for rich material and compresses for weak
material — the skeleton is never announced. Narrative stretches are strictly
chronological, with explicit meta-timing of pacing as a joke ("And this is where
the movie peaks. No joke. This was the climax right here. Ten minutes and
twenty-five seconds in."). Boring parts get one clause; insane parts get
interrogated for a minute.

### Tone toward the subject
Affectionate roasting of everything — characters, submitters, himself — but with
a hard sincerity trigger: when something actually scares, moves, or impresses
him, the jokes stop mid-video and the register drops ("I can't explain it. It
wasn't a disturbing or scary killer... It was just vibes." / the Minecraft
nostalgia / "I know how you feel" about the dead dog). That flash of real feeling
is what makes the roasting read as love, not cynicism. He never punches at the
audience; even his roasts of submitters end in belonging ("What's good, Ken?").

### Endings
Rigidly consistent across all five: (1) a wrap-up observation that restates the
bit's conclusion ("as long as I'm not going against an Android, an alien, or a
radioactive lizard, I'm pretty much immortal"), (2) comment bait ("comment down
below which movies you would have survived"), (3) a sequel tease ("I might do a
part two"), (4) optional announcements (Twitch/Discord/charity), (5) the sign-off
"And moral of the story—" cut off by the outro music sting. The sub ask is
always folded into a bit, never a plain request.

### Catchphrases and verbal tics (verbatim, with observed frequency)
- "You know, ..." — opens 5/5 videos.
- "Today, ladies and gentlemen / ladies and gents" — 4/5 premise statements.
- "Y'all know how we do it." — series openers.
- "All right, let's run it." — launch line.
- "And moral of the story—" — every outro, cut off by the sting.
- "cuz" — the universal connector; hundreds of occurrences; starts most
  explanations.
- "y'all" / "bro" / "my boy" / "my girl" / "our boy" — constant address
  vocabulary ("our boy Jason Voorhees"; "our boy — I take that back. He's not my
  boy.").
- "this dude / this man / this girl / this little girl" — pointing at subjects.
- "junk" as mild euphemism ("this junk is some butt," "that junk had my heart
  racing").
- "murked," "packed up" (killed/died), "cooked" (doomed — "am I cooked?", "he's
  cooked"), "whack," "butt" (bad), "peak" ("it's peak"), "fire," "low-key,"
  "hella," "on deck," "off the rip," "W strategy," "crashed out," "playing meta."
- "pull up on" — his verb for approaching/confronting (5+ uses).
- "the crib" — his word for anyone's home (5+ uses).
- "I'm not going to lie / I won't lie / I'ma be honest."
- "I'm serious, y'all." / "No, I'm serious." / "I'm not trolling."
- "It's that easy. / It's too easy. / easy peasy lemon squeezy."
- "Hear me out." — quoted from imagined commenters, then refused: "No, bro. I'm
  not hearing you out."
- "Pause." — self-flagged joke beat.
- "buddy" — sarcastic address to characters who make horror-movie mistakes
  ("I thought you were in danger, buddy." / "I warned you, buddy.").
- "Boom." / "Bam." — plan-step completions.
- Editor banter as a running bit ("shout out to Mr. Editor" / answering his own
  inserted editor joke on mic).

## Adopting this voice for manhwa recap narration

### (a) What to ADOPT
1. **The joke grammar above, in order of value:** fake-out detonation ("Nah, he
   didn't hesitate. He ate it."); pre-argued objection ("You're probably thinking
   he'll share the loot. Don't be silly."); rhetorical question chains that
   interrogate a character's logic; escalation triples; fake precision ("won
   seventeen of them. To be specific."); fake credentials of story characters
   ("a hide-and-seek champion of the sect, allegedly"); self-interruption where
   the plan collapses mid-sentence.
2. **Rhythm:** rambling "cuz"-connected setups, then SHORT landing sentences.
   The punchline is always the shortest sentence in the entry.
3. **Bit commitment:** treat one ridiculous thesis per entry (or per run of
   entries) with analyst seriousness — a plan, a ranking, a verdict — and never
   wink at the audience inside it.
4. **Sincerity trigger:** drop the jokes for exactly one entry when something
   genuinely lands (a death, a sacrifice, a power reveal that deserves awe). The
   contrast is the weapon.
5. **Affection rule:** roast characters for their choices, never with contempt.
   "this dude really..." / "bro really thought..." energy, not mockery of the
   story itself.
6. **Repeat-for-emphasis doubling** inside a sentence ("he was ready. READY
   ready.") — sparingly, one per chapter.
7. **Refrains:** one short recurring line per chapter ("There is something wrong
   with this little girl" pattern) reused at 2–3 beats for structure.
8. **Address vocabulary mapping:** "our boy / our girl / bro / my guy" already in
   our voice; add "buddy" as sarcastic address to characters making a doomed
   choice, and "cooked" as the doom word.

### (b) What NOT to adopt
1. **No meta/production language.** His "the visuals," "the acting," "the CGI,"
   "10 minutes and 25 seconds in," "12 minutes into the movie," "Warner Bros,
   this is fair use" are all banned for us — narration stays diegetic (no
   panel/caption/narrator/chapter-counting words, per the pipeline rules).
2. **No direct audience address.** "Y'all," "comment down below," sub asks,
   outro stings, part-two teases — our narration is a story voice, not a
   channel voice. Convert audience frames into in-world ones ("anyone watching
   him would think..." → "even his shadow could see what was coming").
3. **No movie/creator references.** His jokes ride on RoboCop, Minecraft,
   Fortnite, Game Theory, kill-count channels. Our jokes must live inside the
   manhwa's world; modern-Earth analogies only if generic ("gamer," "minimum
   wage," "group project" — already proven in our existing style notes).
4. **No fragment entries.** His one-word punchlines ("Boom." / "Surely.") must be
   absorbed into full flowing sentences a TTS voice can read; our entries are
   15–45 words, complete, no colon lead-ins.
5. **No self-insert fantasy plots.** His "here's MY strategy" monologues span a
   whole segment; ours can only borrow the GRAMMAR (confident plan → collapse →
   worse plan) attributed to a character, not to a narrator "I."
6. **His profanity ceiling stays where our current voice is** ("get his ass
   kicked" level) — he swears harder on camera; we don't.
7. **No roasting submitters/real people or channel-business beats** (charity
   counts, Discord, Twitch) — nothing to map to.

### (c) Translation patterns (generic recap line → DerekFDB-voiced, manhwa setting)
Each shows one named pattern; all are diegetic, flowing, TTS-safe.

1. **Fake-out detonation.** Plain: "The disciple asks his master for help, but
   the master is gone." → "Now this was the moment the old master would swoop in
   and save his disciple for sure. Nah. The old man kept sipping his tea."
2. **Pre-argued objection.** Plain: "The MC keeps all the treasure for himself."
   → "And you know he thought about splitting the loot with the party. Don't be
   silly. He took the sword, the ring, AND the map."
3. **Rhetorical question chain.** Plain: "The merchant's prices are absurd." →
   "Fifty gold for one healing potion. Who's buying this? The guild? With whose
   money? The man doesn't even do a disciples' discount. This dude is a scammer,
   and business is booming."
4. **Fake precision + fake credentials.** Plain: "The drunk uncle claims he was
   a famous duelist." → "Uncle Bone claims he won a hundred duels back in his
   prime. Six, to be specific. And two of those were against a fence post."
5. **Self-interruption collapse.** Plain: "He tries to sneak past the guard, but
   fails." → "So the plan was simple: wait for nightfall, step over the sleeping
   guard, take the key. The guard was not sleeping. The plan was now improv."
6. **Escalation triple.** Plain: "The MC has terrible luck." → "The universe
   trucked him, dropped him in a tutorial, and made the tutorial graded. At this
   point the heavens are just bullying him personally."
7. **Sarcasm via "buddy" + "cooked."** Plain: "The arrogant rival challenges the
   MC and loses instantly." → "This dude spent three chapters hyping his family's
   secret art just to get folded in one exchange. The math was never mathing,
   buddy. He was cooked the moment he opened his mouth."
8. **Sincerity drop.** Plain: "The side character dies protecting the MC." →
   "He took the blade that was never meant for him, and he smiled while he did
   it. No joke to make here. Our boy deserved better."
